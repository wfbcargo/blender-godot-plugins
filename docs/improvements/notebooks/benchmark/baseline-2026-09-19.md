# Benchmark baseline, 2026-09-19 (the first time it has been run)

The benchmark defined in `NEXT.md` ("a new human in under 30 s that looks great") had never been run: there was
no notebook under `notebooks/`, and five branches had shipped without one. This is its first scoreboard, taken
straight after Step 2 closed (humanform 0.15.0, lookdev 0.11.0, character-pipeline 0.15.0, follow-through 0.10.0,
rig-anything 0.26.0, wardrobe 0.5.2).

Run by hand, not through the `plugin-round` workflow (the user had not opted into multi-agent orchestration);
the three look critics were independent subagents, each given the question bank before any image was opened.

## Method

One scratch project per character (`tools/scratch_project.py --who ""`), each with its own copy of the humanform
library. The library holds only the five `reference` bodies (Mara, Otto, Juno, Kade, Wren) and no body for any of
these briefs, so every cold build starts cold; the copy is restored from a pristine snapshot before each cold run,
together with the blend and the export, so runs 2 and 3 cannot warm-start on run 1's fitted body.

`~/.claude/skills` was verified identical to `main` for all seven plugins before starting (ignoring line endings
and `.install.json`), so building against the checkout measures what an installed user gets.

Specs were written from the brief alone, choosing only what the benchmark says a user would choose: the body
brief, hair, study_man's move roles, and an outfit if the brief mentions clothing. No library part ids, no
hand-marked flesh zones, no tuned overrides.

## The headline: nothing reached 30 s, and nothing built without a workaround

| character | cold builds (s) | **median** | vs 30 s | warm | after a one-line `[flesh]` edit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bench_marco | 39.3 / 44.3 / 42.2 | **42.2** | +41 % | 2.1 s | 15.1 s |
| bench_mei | 36.7 / 33.0 / 31.1 | **33.0*** | +10 %* | 2.0 s | 7.4 s |
| bench_ruth | 45.5 / 41.7 / 40.5 | **41.7** | +39 % | 2.1 s | 14.9 s |

*bench_mei's builds exclude the review stage (`to=export`), because review fails on her - see finding 4. The
other two spend 7.0-8.3 s there, so her comparable figure is about **41 s**. All three are ~40 s, not 30 s.

**The resume path is excellent and is not the problem**: a rebuild with nothing changed is 2.0-2.1 s, and a
one-line flesh edit is 7-15 s. Cold build is where the target is missed.

### Slowest stages (median run)

| character | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|
| bench_marco | bake 9.8 | moves 8.8 | review 8.2 |
| bench_mei | bake 10.0 | moves 9.2 | export 5.0 |
| bench_ruth | body 9.5 | bake 9.0 | moves 7.5 |

`bake` and `moves` are the two constants, 8-10 s each. Ruth's `body` is 9.2-9.9 s against Marco's 5.4-6.3 and
Mei's 4.6-5.0 - her age is outside ANSUR's range (finding 5), so her fit has no near neighbour to start from.
Cutting the target needs bake and moves; nothing else is close.

## Findings: not one of the three briefs built on the first attempt

Each spec was written clean and run. Every one failed, and each needed a documented workaround before it
produced a character. **This is the benchmark's real result** - the time numbers above are all measured on
specs that already carry workarounds.

### 1. `Run` fails its clip checks, and it is the walk criterion doing it (Marco, Mei)

```
moves: clips failing their checks: ['Run'] (list a role in moves.may_fail to export it forced)
```

From the manifest's `known_failures`:

```
upper_arm.L: the arm swings 53 degrees but never comes back to hanging
             - nearest 2 degrees in front (limit 2) - carried out in front, not swung
```

An arm that never comes back to hanging is what a run **is**. This is 06 rank 5 - `rig-anything` never sets
`U.running`, so every Run is judged as a walk - measured on new characters, and it is not cosmetic: it stops
the build.

The numbers show how arbitrary the gate is. All three run with the same arm swing; only the carry minimum,
against a limit of 2 deg, decides:

| character | stature | Run arm swing | nearest in front | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bench_marco | 1.72 | 53.0 deg | 2.5 deg | FAIL |
| bench_mei | 1.75 | 51.6 deg | 2.1 deg | FAIL |
| bench_ruth | 1.62 | 52.7 deg | 1.2 deg | pass |

Ruth passes by 0.8 deg. Whether a brand-new character can be built is decided by noise against a criterion that
should not be applied to a run at all. **Fix `U.running` first in the motion step.**

### 2. The error names the role but not the reason

`RuntimeError: moves: clips failing their checks: ['Run']` throws away `out[role]["failures"]`, which it has in
hand. The reason above was only recoverable from the exported manifest, after forcing the clip through with
`may_fail`. Put the failures in the message.

### 3. A "stocky" man seeds no belly (Marco)

```
flesh: MISSED belly: 9 seed vertices, fewer than 51 ... the skin stands at most 0.0458 m out
(a seed needs 0.0207 m) and 26% of its lean radius (needs 25%); 55 pass the first, 9 the second, 9 both
```

`build = "heavy"` (BMI 31) on a 1.72 m man, and the belly still does not seed: the two tests pass 55 and 9
vertices but only 9 pass both. Marco needs `may_miss = ["belly"]` - the same workaround `study_woman.toml`
carries, which Step 4 says should not be needed. The benchmark reproduces Step 4 on a third body, and with a
far wider margin than study_woman's 0.0001 m. A user who writes "stocky" and asks for a belly gets no belly.

### 4. Ruth's butt is MISPLACED, and that gate has no escape hatch

```
flesh: regions outside their anatomical zone: butt.L: weight 0.60 10 cm above its apex, over 0.3:
the attachment moves with the mass; butt.R: weight 0.53 10 cm above its apex, over 0.3
```

This is `check_placement`, added in the flesh round to stop breast bones landing on chins. On a `soft` build it
rejects a legitimate body. Unlike `may_miss` and `may_fail` it raises unconditionally - **there is no spec-level
way past it**, so the only options are hand-marked zones or changing the body. Ruth shipped with `butt` dropped
from `types`, i.e. with no butt jiggle at all.

### 5. Mei cannot finish a full build: review calls her hands off-body

```
review: close-up tiles that do not show their part:
["hand_back.L: off_body (no figure at the subject's centroid)", "hand_back.R: off_body ..."]
```

NEXT.md records this for the cast's Mei with the note "build her with `to=export`". It is **not specific to that
character**: it reproduces on a brand-new body from the brief. Her benchmark timings therefore exclude review,
and she is the one character here with no Blender close set to pair against.

## Brief fidelity: what the tools could not say

Every brief named an ancestry and none could be expressed. The other gaps are one per brief.

### Ancestry is plumbed but unreachable (all three briefs)

`scaffold.create_macros` reads `asian`, `caucasian` and `african` straight off the sheet - they are in
`UNFITTED`, and `create_macros` does `out[k] = float(s[k]) if s.get(k) is not None else defaults[k]`. MPFB is
ready for them. But `sheet.new()` has no such parameters, and `[body]` is handed to `sheet.new(**brief)`
unfiltered (spec.py runs no `_unknown` whitelist on `[body]`), so a spec that tries dies at the door:

```
>>> sheet.new(name="X", sex="male", asian=1.0)
TypeError: new() got an unexpected keyword argument 'asian'
```

So "Hispanic", "Asian" and "Caucasian" were each carried by skin and iris colour alone. Two things are needed:
the three macros as sheet fields (small - the layer under them already works), and a decision on what "Hispanic"
maps to, since MPFB offers only asian/caucasian/african.

### Age over 58 is clamped in silence (Ruth)

`ansur_path(62)` returns `aged`, and `create_macros` then sets `age = AGE_RANGE[1]`, i.e. 58. That is the
documented cap. What is not documented is that **`sheet.validate()` returns no problems at all for age 62** -
`AGE_LIMITS` is (1, 90), so the spec is accepted, and the body is quietly built ten years younger than asked.
A brief that says "in her 60s" should at least be told. See the critic's verdict on whether she reads as 60s.

### One `build` field for two words (Mei)

"slim figure, athletic build" is two of `BUILDS`' seven names at once. Written as `build = "slim"` with
`muscle = 0.6`; `build = "athletic"` would have been as defensible and gives a different body.

### "A strong jaw" has no field (Marco)

Nothing in the brief schema shapes a jaw. The cast's hand-built Marco got one from a library face part tagged
"broad lower face", which means knowing part ids - not something a user writing from a brief would do, and not
in the benchmark's list of what a user chooses. Built without it, and the jaw is whatever the fit produced.

### `long_loose` hair (Mei)

Used as the brief's "long hair". Known rigid; see the critic.

## A check that does not match the eye, found by cross-examining the critics

Both critics reported coloured lines along the fingers - the defect `skin-finger-edges` (humanform 0.15.0,
lookdev 0.10.0) shipped a fix for. Rather than take their word, the shipped measurements were run on the
same glbs. They disagree with the critics, and with each other:

| | clear_midday hand_back (what the critics complain about) | overcast palms |
|---|---|---|
| `edges --kind transmittance` | 0 lines, 0.00‰ on every tile - "clean" | 0 lines |
| `edges --kind specular` | 0 lines, 0.00‰ on every tile | **PALE_LINES: Marco 2 tiles, Ruth 1** |
| the two critics | "warm orange rim along every finger silhouette" | "pale rim lines" |

Three things follow.

1. **`edges --kind specular` fails on both brand-new characters.** Marco's palms read 0.36‰ and 0.37‰ against a
   0.1‰ limit under overcast (worst 1.05x), Ruth's palm 0.19‰. NEXT.md already noted this check is
   "selftest-only" and that its 0.4 ratio "sits just above a thigh-rim sheen at 0.35x"; on fresh characters it
   now fires for real. It is not in `regress`, so nothing would have caught it.
2. **The clear_midday finger lines the critics both see are measured as zero by both kinds.** Two independent
   critics, two different characters, the same complaint, and the check says clean. Either the measurement
   misses the silhouette or the critics are reading something else as a line.
3. **The most likely answer is that they are reading a feature as a defect.** `skin-finger-edges` deliberately
   *kept* "the backlit glow in the finger webs" - warm translucency at a lit finger edge is what real skin does.
   If so the bug is in the question bank, not the skin: LOOK-S4 asks for "no coloured lines" without telling a
   critic how to separate a correct warm translucent edge from a hard orange line. **Worth settling before any
   more skin work**, because right now the eye and the number cannot both be trusted and there is no tie-breaker.

The two critics also disagreed on where the lines come from - Ruth's said they are stronger in the Blender tile
and therefore baked, Marco's said Blender has none and Godot introduces them. That disagreement is itself the
finding: nobody can currently tell, from the shipped outputs, which side of the pipeline puts them there.

## The critics: none of the three reads as a real person

Three independent subagents, one per character, each given `lookdev/references/critic-look.md` and required to
write its questions before opening an image. None had built anything.

| character | pass count | 1 m | full body |
|---|---|---|---|
| bench_marco | 11 / 19 | no | no |
| bench_mei | 11 / 18 | no | no |
| bench_ruth | 10 / 16 | no | no |

### Four questions failed on all three characters

- **LOOK-H1** hair reads as a helmet, not hair.
- **LOOK-H2** the hairline is a hard cut or a stencilled spiky fringe.
- **LOOK-E3** the irises have no depth - no limbal ring, a flat disc pupil.
- **LOOK-F2** faceting, lumps or seams somewhere in the set.

All three critics independently put **the eyes** and **the hair** in their top three. That is the strongest
signal this benchmark produced, and neither is a lighting problem.

### The eyes are lost in transit, not missing from the model

Marco's critic checked the paired Blender tile: `close/eyes.png` **has** a catchlight and iris shading, and the
Godot tile does not. Ruth's critic saw the same asymmetry from the other side - her Godot eyes sit in "a muddy
brown AO pit that the Blender tile does not have". Mei's had no Blender pair and reported the iris as "a flat
blob with straight, hard bottom and side edges - a rectangular texture patch, not a circle".

So this is a Blender -> Godot transfer defect in the eye material, which makes it the cheapest of the top-three
gaps to fix and the most damaging to leave: every critic said the dead eyes are what kills the 1 m read.

### Age is not built at all

Marco is specified 44 and reads "late teens to early twenties" - no nasolabial fold, no beard shadow, no skin
slack. Ruth is specified 62 and reads "about 35, wearing grey hair"; her critic listed what is absent: jowl,
neck sag, lid hooding, crow's feet, lip thinning, hand tendons, age spots, brow thinning, posture.

**The 58-year fit cap is not the blocker.** As that critic put it, none of those is a measurement - they are all
authorable on top of a 58-year fit, and none was attempted. There is no age layer, only an age number.

### Ancestry was legible on none of them

Expected, given it cannot be expressed (above), but worth the number: Marco "reads closest to a generic East
Asian base mesh with the tone dialled warm" for a Hispanic brief; Mei's skin came out hue 25.3 deg, a pink-peach,
where East Asian skin sits nearer 30-40 deg. Mei's critic traced it properly: the manifest's `brief` struct has
no ethnicity field, so the word was dropped at brief-to-spec and survived only as two RGB values - one of which
I chose wrong. Colour alone cannot carry it, and the skin value is a hand guess with nothing to check it against.

### `long_loose` is not long

NEXT.md records `long_loose` as "still rigid". It is worse than that: on Mei it **stops at the shoulder**. Her
critic scanned the rows of `overcast_full.png` - the hair mass ends at y=196 and the shoulders start at y=198 -
and called the result "a blunt bob with a straight-cut fringe", plus a hard cap seam across the crown, the ear
covered by a flat plane, and a detached faceted shard on the shoulder. A brief that says "long hair" does not
get long hair from the only long preset.

### One critic claim checked and rejected

Mei's critic read `build.skipped: ["bake", "body", "hair", "moves"]` in the manifest and concluded her hair was
"reused from cache and not rebuilt for this character". That manifest was written by the **flesh-edit rebuild**,
not the cold build - skipping unchanged stages is exactly what resume is meant to do. The cold runs all built
hair (0.7-0.8 s each). The hair is genuinely a shoulder-length bob; it was not inherited from another character.

Worth noting for future rounds: the glbs the critics judged were last written by the flesh-edit rebuild, so a
manifest read alone cannot tell a critic which build produced the asset it is looking at.

## What this changes

The benchmark was run before the motion step in order to find out whether motion was the right priority. It
half-confirms and half-redirects.

**Confirms:** `U.running` (06 rank 5, the first item of Step 5) is the single most disruptive bug found here.
It is not the cosmetic "runs are judged as walks" it was filed as - it **stops a new character building at
all**, on 2 of 3 briefs, and the third escaped by 0.8 degrees. Whatever else happens, that goes first, and the
motion step is where it lives.

**Redirects:** the look verdicts do not point at motion. All three critics put **the eyes** and **the hair** in
their top three, and nothing else came close. The eyes are a Blender -> Godot transfer loss with the catchlight
present on one side and absent on the other, which is a small, well-located fix with a large payoff. Stiff
walking was not what any critic named; they could not get past the face.

Ranked by evidence gathered here, not by the order in NEXT.md:

1. **`U.running`** - blocks builds, 2 of 3, and it is already Step 5's first item.
2. **Eyes: the catchlight and iris depth lost between Blender and Godot** - named by all three critics, located
   to one side of the pipeline, small.
3. **Hair** - `long_loose` is not long, `short_crop` reads as a moulded cap on both bodies that used it, and
   every critic put it top three.
4. **An age layer** - two briefs asked for 40s and 60s and both read twenty-plus years young. The 58-year fit
   cap is not what is stopping it.
5. **Ancestry as a sheet field** - three briefs named one, none was legible; the macro layer under it already
   works, so this is a small change plus a decision about "Hispanic".
6. **Cold build time** - ~40 s against 30 s, all in `bake` (8-10 s) and `moves` (7-10 s). The resume path is
   already excellent at 2 s and needs nothing.
7. **The flesh gates** - all three briefs needed a `may_miss`, a `may_fail` or a dropped type. `MISPLACED` has
   no escape hatch at all.
8. **`edges --kind specular` fails on fresh characters and is not in regress**, and LOOK-S4 cannot currently
   separate a correct warm translucent finger edge from a defect.

Re-run this benchmark after each round with the same three briefs and the same method, and compare the two
numbers that matter: the cold median against 30 s, and the critic pass counts (11/19, 11/18, 10/16 today).

## What the baseline led to, the same day

The two items this benchmark ranked first were taken straight away.

**1. `U.running` (rig-anything 0.27.0, shipped).** The run branch of `verify.arm_swing` had always existed -
its docstring says "a run legitimately keeps both arms in front (every shipped run comes no nearer hanging
than 1.2 degrees)" - and never ran, because `Upper` did not carry the flag and the caller read it as
`getattr(U, "running", False)`. `bench_marco` now builds with `may_fail = ["Run"]` removed.

Regress had not caught it, and could not have: `pipeline_woman`, the only fixture with a Run, carries
[1.1, 53.2] and passed the walk criterion by 0.9 degrees. The manifest now records `arm_gait` per clip so
the flag cannot go quiet again.

**2. The eye material preset (lookdev 0.12.0, humanform 0.16.0, shipped).** There was no eye material at
all; the critics' "lost between Blender and Godot" was wrong, and so was this notebook's first reading of
it. The preset adds a cornea, a limbal ring and a pupil floor. Measured on the figures after the rebuild,
iris-over-pupil went from 2.14x (Marco) to 6.3x, and the benchmark's own 1.35x / 2.14x / 6.97x line is what
set the 6x floor.

Its new check, `lookdev eyes`, failed on its first regress run for a reason that was not the change:
`pipeline_woman` never put `LD_SCRIPTS` on `sys.path`, so the fixture had been building its eyes - and its
brows and lashes, which import lookdev the same way - through the flat fallback, unnoticed, because nothing
had ever looked at an eye.

**Still open from this baseline**, in the order the evidence supports: hair (every critic's top three;
`long_loose` is not long), the rest of Step 5, an age layer, ancestry as a sheet field, cold build time
(~40 s against 30 s, in `bake` and `moves`), the flesh gates, and `edges --kind specular` failing on fresh
characters while not being in regress.

**Re-run this benchmark before the next round** with the same three briefs and the same method. Today's
numbers to beat: cold median 42.2 / ~41 / 41.7 s, and critic pass counts 11/19, 11/18, 10/16.

## The eye preset, judged by a fresh critic - and one fix that was tried and rejected

An independent critic (which had not built anything) judged the rebuilt `cast_marco`, told what the previous
round had complained about and asked to answer independently.

**Answered:** a distinct pupil separate from the iris, and a limbal ring - "the previous critics' 'flat disc
pupil in a dark blob' and 'flat faceted polygon' no longer hold". The ring reads at the nasal and temporal
edges and fades across the bottom.

**Not answered - the catchlight, in 2 of 3 presets.** Measured in the cornea: clear_midday peak 145/146 and
overcast 141/139, both of which are lid skin, not the eye; golden_hour 217 on the **left eye only**, 122 on
the right. The critic's reading is right and worth keeping: **overcast is one enormous softbox and should give
the largest, most reliable catchlight of the three, and it gives none.** So the cornea has a tight specular
lobe off the sun and essentially no environment reflection. That is the next piece of eye work, and it is a
lighting/reflection question (a reflection probe, or sky specular reaching a recessed eyeball), not a
material-colour one.

### Rejected: shrinking the iris and pupil to life size

The critic also measured the iris at ~17.5 mm against a real 11.5-12 mm and the pupil at ~9.4 mm against 2-4 mm
in sun, and called it a doll-eye tell. **The measurement is correct.** Confirmed on the asset itself: MPFB's
eye proxy is **33.3 mm across** (a real eyeball is ~24 mm), so humanform's original 32 and 14 degree
half-angles - whose docstring claims "~11-12 mm across", which is arithmetically false - draw a 17.65 mm iris
and an 8.06 mm pupil. With `rings = 32` the face rings land at 3.73, 8.40, 14.00, 19.60, 25.21 and 30.82
degrees, i.e. 2.17, 4.88, 8.08, 11.20, 14.23 and 17.11 mm, so life size was available exactly.

**It was tried (iris 20.6 deg, pupil 6.9 deg = 11.8 mm and 4.0 mm) and it looks worse.** The rendered eye reads
as a small iris floating in a wide opening - a startled doll stare - while the shipped 17.6 mm iris fills the
aperture and touches the upper lid, which is what a human eye does.

The reason is that **the eyelids are MPFB's and are fitted to its oversized ball**. What an eye reads by is the
iris against the palpebral aperture, not absolute millimetres, and at 33.3 mm the oversized iris happens to
land on the right ratio. Correcting the iris alone breaks the only thing that was right.

So the real defect is one level down - the eyeball proxy is ~39% oversized and the lids follow it - and fixing
it means scaling the ball and the aperture together in humanform, not changing two angles in a material preset.
A flatter-than-real cornea is also part of why the catchlight is so hard to catch. **Do not re-try the angle
change on its own**; the branch was dropped rather than shipped.
