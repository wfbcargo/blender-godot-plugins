# Benchmark re-run, 2026-09-21 (after the motion, asymmetry and loose-cloth rounds)

The second full run of the benchmark, with the same three briefs and the same method as
[baseline-2026-09-19.md](baseline-2026-09-19.md). Taken on humanform 0.17.0, rig-anything 0.38.0,
character-pipeline 0.16.0, follow-through 0.10.0, wardrobe 0.7.0 and lookdev 0.12.0. `~/.claude/skills` was
checked identical to `main` for all seven plugins before starting (`diff -r --strip-trailing-cr`, ignoring
`__pycache__` and `.install.json`).

Run by hand, not through `plugin-round`. The three look critics were independent subagents, each told to
write its questions from `critic-look.md` before opening any image.

## Method, and one change to it

The same as the baseline: one scratch project per character (`tools/scratch_project.py --who ""`). The
humanform library holds only the five `reference` bodies plus parts. Before every cold run, a small script
(`cold.sh`) restores that library from a pristine snapshot and deletes the blend and the export, then times
`build.sh <id> fresh=1` with `date`, Blender start-up included. The specs were written from the brief alone
(below). Nothing was asked for that a user would not choose.

**Another session's CPU load contaminated the timings, and part of this run could not be taken clean.** A
second Claude session on the same machine was running a CPU-bound embedding eval (`semantic-eval.ts`),
starting new runs one after another, each one pinning every core. It started during Ruth's builds:

- Her first three cold runs came in at 63.9, 44.0 and 81.2 s, with every stage slower together (bake 9.6 ->
  20.2, review 7.4 -> 17.8). That is the signature of load, not code.
- A second batch waited for the first eval to exit (48.6 s, clean), then collided with the next eval (62.4,
  96.4).
- A third batch gated each build on 20 s of system load under 25%. It still started in the gaps between
  evals and measured 195 s.

**Ruth's figure below is the median of the two runs that ran between evals.** It is provisional. Marco's and
Mei's six runs all finished before 11:10, before any of this started, and agree with each other within 2 s.

For next time: check `Get-Process` / `LoadPercentage` before starting, and time on a machine nothing else is
using. A gate on sampled load is not enough when the other job restarts itself.

**`moves` is now the single largest stage, 11-13 s, up from 7.5-9.2 s at baseline**, even with 0.37.0's

cut, not one.**

Fixed since baseline:

- Mei's `hand_back` off_body false alarm: gone; her full build passes review.
- Marco's belly seeds on the brief's `heavy` build.

Still open, and now the only thing stopping two of three briefs building clean:

### 1. `check_placement` still has no escape hatch, and it now catches Mei by 0.01

```
flesh: MISPLACED butt.L: weight 0.89 at its apex (the tail), under 0.9
```

This is a different rule from Ruth's. It is the tail-weight floor that B+C (follow-through 0.8.0) added,
failing on one side only, by 0.01, and it is deterministic (reproduced on a second cold run). Ruth fails the
attachment rule exactly as at baseline:

```
butt.L: weight 0.67 10 cm above its apex, over 0.3; butt.R: weight 0.55 10 cm above its apex, over 0.3
```

(0.60 / 0.53 at baseline.)

**Both women ship with no butt jiggle,** because dropping the type is the only spec-level way past it.
This is the baseline's finding 4, now on two of three briefs. It needs either an escape (`may_misplace`,
matching `may_miss` and `may_fail`) or a placement that holds on these bodies. On Mei's evidence, a
one-sided 0.01 miss is noise against a hard floor, just as the Run arm-carry gate was.

### 2. A `soft` 62-year-old woman seeds no belly

```
flesh: MISSED belly: ... the skin stands at most 0.0234 m out (a seed needs 0.0195 m) and 16% of its lean
radius (needs 25%); 3 vertices pass the first, 0 the second, 0 both (a region needs 55)
```

The brief says "a little heavier". `cast_ruth.toml` carries the same `may_miss`, so this is known, but the
benchmark confirms it on a body built fresh from the brief. Step 4 (flesh correctness) still owns it.

*Ruth's critic answered 12/18 and failed LOOK-H4 (stipple in the neck shadow). Measured, that claim does not
hold: `lookdev stipple clear_midday_head_side.png --region 250,440,180,150` gives lattice 0.114 against a
0.25 limit, clean. The grain it saw is the skin detail (finding 3 below), not shadow dither, so H4 is
counted as a pass.

The question banks differ slightly between runs (16-19 answered), so read these as ratios: roughly 59-63%
at baseline, now 65-72%.

### Failed on all three, as at baseline

- **LOOK-H1 / H2, the hair.** Still a helmet shell with a comb-toothed hairline on all three. Every critic
  put it first. `long_loose` on Mei is still shoulder length ("hair ends at the shoulder line"), with a
  flap at the crown and a torn strip behind the neck. Marco's `short_crop` has a hard black strip in front
  of each ear down to the jaw (H3). Ruth's reads as a striped grey bowl cut.
- **LOOK-E3, the eyes.** All three still say "dead": flat pupils and no catchlight in Godot. This is the
  catchlight the eye-material critic already located (no environment reflection in the cornea), still
  open. Mei's critic also says the limbal ring barely reads.
- **LOOK-S4, fingertip rims.** Orange-red on Mei and Ruth (stronger in the Blender tile), and pale glassy
  grey on Marco's nails. The last is the `edges --kind specular` failure the baseline found and that is
  still not in regress.

### New: a cell pattern on the forearms and hands (all three critics, confirmed)

All three critics independently describe a "reptile-scale", "snakeskin" or "pebbled" regular cell texture
over the forearms, wrists, hands and thighs, with cells about 3-5 mm across, at the 0.5 m hand tiles. I
looked at `bench_marco/look/clear_midday_hand_back.L.png` myself and it is plainly there on the forearm. It
is most likely the Step 2 two-octave skin detail (lookdev 0.9.0) at a scale set for the face, reading as
cells on the limbs under a raking sun.

The Step 2 check, `grain --region cheek`, only ever measures the cheek, so nothing tests the limbs. **Add a
limb region to `grain` (or a cell-pattern check) with a control, then fix the scale per region.** The
baseline critics did not report this, so either it arrived with a later change or the hand tiles got
closer. Worth a bisect on one character before fixing.

### New: Ruth's full tile is past white under clear_midday

close-shot exits 1 on `SKIN_PAST_WHITE: 3.91% of the figure ... (max 1%)` for `clear_midday full`. It is a
real check failing on a real character: pale skin and grey hair under the midday key. Overcast is 0.00%.
Either the preset's exposure has too little headroom for fair skin or the check's threshold was set on
darker figures. Look at it before either is changed.

### Brief fidelity: unchanged from baseline

- **Age: none of the three reads its age.** Marco (44) reads early-to-mid 20s. Ruth (62) reads 30s-40s,
  with no nasolabial folds, crow's feet, jowls or neck sag. The age layer is still the most-requested
  missing feature after hair.
- **Ancestry is still not expressible.** Marco reads East Asian rather than Hispanic. Mei reads "ambiguous
  or mixed". Ruth's pale Caucasian reads correctly, which is the one case skin colour can carry alone.
- **Build:** Marco reads soft and heavy, not stocky (`heavy` has no broad-frame or thick-neck term).
  Mei's `slim` with muscle 0.6 reads pear-shaped, not skinny or athletic. Ruth's `soft` reads right.
- **Jaw:** Marco's "strong jaw" has no field, and it reads round and soft.

## What this changes

The ranking from the baseline mostly stands. Re-ordered by this run's evidence:

1. **Hair.** Every critic's first item, on both runs, on all six characters judged.
2. **The flesh gates.** They are now the only thing stopping two of three briefs building clean. A
   `may_misplace` escape is small; the placement itself is Step 4.
3. **The limb skin cells.** New, seen by all three critics, confirmed by eye, and unmeasured. Build the
   instrument first.
4. **The eye catchlight** (a reflection or sky-specular question) and **an age layer**.
5. **Cold build time.** `moves` has grown to 11-13 s and is now the largest stage. Draft-quality moves and
   skipping the gait report on Idle and the turns are the next cuts, and bake and review both need one too.
6. **Ancestry as a sheet field** and **`edges --kind specular` into regress**, both unchanged.

Numbers to beat next time: cold median 43.5 / 42.3 / ~46 s (Ruth provisional), critic pass counts 12/18,
11/17, 13/18.
